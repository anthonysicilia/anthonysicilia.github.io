I study how large language models and other AI systems reason when information is uncertain, goals are unclear, or people are part of the loop. My work combines statistical tools for uncertainty with linguistic theories of ambiguity, context, and communication. In this view, language can become a computational interface between uncertain evidence and useful action.

Selected Research Directions

Uncertainty and forecasting

How can language models represent uncertainty in their knowledge, future outcomes, and other people's beliefs?

Examples: Forecasting outcomes in negotiations, on social media, and predicting when users are uncertain.

Collaboration and ambiguous goals

How should AI systems ask questions, repair misunderstandings, and refine goals before producing an answer or taking action?

Examples: Resolving ambiguous user goals in scientific coding workflows and forming collaborative plans with agentic AI systems.

Learning from imperfect data

How can statistical learning theory help us reason about bias, robustness, and effective use of human supervision when data are noisy or limited?

Examples: Achieving more fair human-AI conversations and learning from limited data in safety-critical scenarios.

Multimodal reasoning

How should AI systems reason when language connects to images, signed language, interfaces, instructions, and embodied context?

Examples: Building multimodal and accessible interfaces to AI systems.
